About this report — the simulation & stress test behind it

What was tested. A full end-to-end simulation grew the map from zero to 1.5M users across the 10 festival days, driven entirely by phone-to-phone (street-team) invitations — one attendee touches another's phone, they scan a QR, they're in, and now they invite too. Every simulated user walks the site: every Platz, potty, shop, first-aid station, and stage, taking photos and firing HyperPosts. The two-weekend Friday/Saturday spikes are modeled explicitly as the high-use scenario.

What it produced. The occupancy and engagement figures above are the direct output of that simulation — ~ attendees, ~ unique map-users, restroom uses, vendor pass-bys, photos, and HyperPosts over ten days.

Where the system strains (and the fix). The stress test also pushed the platform to failure. At festival scale the functional build is correct, but the real-time growth features — the street-team leaderboard and status polling — saturate first: a leaderboard aggregate polled every few seconds by every recruiter overwhelms the database at only a few hundred concurrent recruiters, well before the crowd numbers above. The remedy is standard festival-hardening: replace polling with push/cached counters, lighten the occupancy ping cadence, and scale the data tier. With those applied, the simulation survives the full 1.5M curve across both peak Saturdays at roughly a third of the hardened ceiling. Full breakdown in the engineering load-test report.

How the numbers are made. The map runs on phone, tablet, and computer with no app-store install and no signal required. Presence + dwell rules turn movement into events: ≥2 min in a restroom = a potty use; ≥3 min at first aid = a visit; a 15 m frontage crossing = a vendor pass-by; presence in a stage geofence during a set = act draw. These are synthetic sample figures sized to a realistic MusikFest to illustrate the daily report ArtsQuest would receive.
